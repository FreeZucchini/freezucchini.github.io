SPACE SYSTEMS

TVAC Qualification

Testing and debugging a CubeSat payload during thermal-vacuum qualification and flight-readiness testing.

THE PROJECT

What was it?

Before a CubeSat can be flown in space, its hardware must be tested under the environmental conditions it will experience during the mission. APSS-2 underwent thermal-vacuum (TVAC) qualification to verify that the spacecraft could operate reliably across temperature and pressure extremes.

I worked on the testing and debugging of the APSS-2 payload, including the payload-board test software and sensor calibration. The payload could be operated using functional, automated and manual performance tests, with automated test scripts also used during environmental testing and intended for use during the mission.

MY CONTRIBUTION

What I did

I was responsible for testing and debugging the payload-board test software during the qualification campaign. I worked through the different test modes and helped ensure the payload could be operated reliably before, during and after environmental testing.

I also took responsibility for calibrating the sensors on the flight payload. This involved learning how the payload operated, carrying out measurements and verifying that the sensor readings were suitable for flight. The payload itself was particularly challenging because it contained a significant amount of power electronics.

ENGINEERING CHALLENGE

A challenge I faced

During testing, the payload test software began boot-looping, preventing the qualification tests from running correctly. We investigated the problem and narrowed it down to a voltage drop across the interface cables connecting the payload inside the TVAC chamber to the equipment outside.

I compensated for the voltage drop and we subsequently installed a new interface cable with approximately half the resistance and twice the number of ground paths. This resolved the power issue and allowed testing to continue.

Moving forward we are now preparing for the qualification of our flight payload. I have taken the responsibility of ensuring we have all the interfaces on the umbilical PCB on the APSS-2 to ensure we can collect enough data during environmental testing, charge the battery before launch and also communicate with the onboard-computer.

I am also writing a battery test plan to ensure the battery we have is flight ready.

TAKEAWAY

What I learned

This was my first experience debugging a complete space-system payload during qualification testing. I learned how hardware, software, test equipment and environmental conditions all interact during system-level testing.

I also gained a much better understanding of the importance of structured test plans, good documentation and repeatable automated testing when preparing hardware for flight.
