POWER ELECTRONICS

Lipo Charger

A PCB capable of charging 6 small Lipo's simultaneously.

THE PROJECT

What was it?

During our annual pico-satellite competition teams would charge their batteries one at a time. This was inefficient and teams lost precious time to test their payloads before launch. I developed this PCB to charge six LiPo batteries simultaneously, allowing teams to charge their batteries in parallel.

TECHNICAL CONCEPTS

What was involved?

  • USB-C Power Delivery
  • Power-delivery ICs
  • High-current PCB routing
  • Battery charging
  • Circular PCB layout
  • Hardware testing

MY CONTRIBUTION

What I did

When I took on this project it was just an idea, no schematic, and no prior research. I started off by researching a Power Delivery IC that could deliver sufficient current for six LiPo batteries while not requiring a microcontroller for configuration. I selected the AP33771CFBZ-13-FA01 IC because we can change the output current and voltage of the PD negotiation just by changing two resistors. I then made the schematic and designed the PCB. I decided to make this a circular PCB and learned how to use Altium's polar grid.

ENGINEERING CHALLENGE

A challenge I faced

I was having trouble routing the USB-C connector to the AP33771CFBZ-13-FA01 in Altium. I was struggling to route the USB-C connector to the AP33771CFBZ-13-FA01 while maintaining the required CC connections. I ultimately used two vias to route the CC connection, allowing the rest of the board layout to remain compact.

TAKEAWAY

What I learned

This project was my first introduction to Power Electronics both in terms of circuit and PCB design. I really enjoyed learning about different USB protocols when looking into the USB C connector, and I also got a chance to broaden my PCB design experience by routing a circular PCB using the Polar Grid in Altium.
